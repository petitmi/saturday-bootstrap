## 3. Grammar & Phrasing Corrections

**1.**  
Original: "The story is about the mountain, and the mountain is in the north areas in Vancouver."  
Correction: "The story is about a mountain in the northern area of Vancouver."  
Why: "Areas" should be singular "area" (or "northern part"); avoid redundant "the mountain...and the mountain."

**2.**  
Original: "I'm going to say the story is about a local squirmish legend."  
Correction: "I'm going to tell you about a local Squamish legend."  
Why: "Tell" is more natural than "say" for narrating; "Squamish" is a proper noun (capitalize); avoid "I'm going to say" redundancy.

**3.**  
Original: "And we'll step into the story itself."  
Correction: "Let's dive into the story itself."  
Why: "Step into" is awkward in this context; "dive into" is the natural idiom for beginning a narrative.

**4.**  
Original: "I found the form is really weird, and I want to share it to you."  
Correction: "I found the form really weird, and I want to share it with you."  
Why: "Share [something] with [someone]" is the correct preposition; "found [noun] [adjective]" doesn't need "is."

**5.**  
Original: "you can see two shark mountains."  
Correction: "you can see two sharp mountains." *(assuming voice-to-text error for "sharp")*  
Why: "Shark" is likely a transcription error; if you meant "sharp," it describes pointed peaks naturally.

**6.**  
Original: "in the local square mesh story, they call the two sisters."  
Correction: "in the local Squamish story, they are called the Two Sisters."  
Why: Passive voice "are called" is more natural; "Squamish" spelling; capitalize "Two Sisters" as a proper name.

**7.**  
Original: "And the two theaters. It's a person, a two-person."  
Correction: "The two sisters were two people."  
Why: "Theaters" is a transcription error (should be "sisters"); "It's a person, a two-person" is grammatically broken—use "were two people."

**8.**  
Original: "So their behaviors… Finally, makes them become the two mountains."  
Correction: "Their actions ultimately caused them to become the two mountains."  
Why: Subject-verb agreement: "behaviors...make" (plural) not "makes"; "ultimately" is more precise than "finally"; "caused them to become" is clearer than "makes them become."

**9.**  
Original: "I mean, something in the local square mesh legend. They were called as this or the transfers."  
Correction: "In the local Squamish legends, beings called the Transformers have this power."  
Why: Fragment error; "called as" is incorrect (use "called" or "known as"); "Transformers" should be capitalized if it's a specific entity name.

**10.**  
Original: "They have truly. Uh, amazing power, and they… transferred to settlers into the mountains."  
Correction: "They have truly amazing power, and they transformed people into mountains."  
Why: Don't split "truly amazing" with a pause/fragment; "transformed [object] into [result]" not "transferred to settlers into."

## 4. Storytelling & Narrative Impact

### The Hook & Opening
You open with "a weird story I just read last week" and mention Cleveland Dam, which is moderately engaging but vague. **Strengthen it:** Start with a vivid image or question—e.g., *"Imagine two mountains watching over Vancouver for centuries—still alive, still aware, but frozen in stone. This is the Squamish legend of the Two Sisters."* This immediately establishes intrigue and stakes.

### Pacing & Structure (Macro-Level Story Beats)
**Beginning:** You introduce the Two Sisters legend adequately but take too long getting to the core (Cleveland Dam, map references before the story).  
**Middle:** You pivot to the philosophical question *"Who are the Transformers?"* which is interesting, but this shift feels abrupt. The narrative then splits into three threads: (1) Transformer identity, (2) the horror of being transformed yet conscious, (3) the geographical/memory function of the stories. These ideas compete rather than build on each other.  
**End:** There is no clear conclusion. The presentation dissolves into Q&A about whether transformed beings are happy, then a tangent about the salmon story, then trailing off. **Fix:** Bring the threads together—e.g., *"These stories aren't just myths; they're maps, moral lessons, and maybe warnings about the cost of immortality."*

**Where it drags:** The repeated clarifications about "Transformers vs. gods" and the back-and-forth about pronunciation slow momentum.  
**Where it rushes:** The salmon story at the end is introduced too quickly and feels like an afterthought rather than a planned comparison.

### Emotional Resonance & Details
You touch on a genuinely chilling idea: *"after the person was transformed into other forms, they're still alive...they can think, they can understand the world...they just not can to speak."* This is vivid **show-don't-tell**—excellent. However, you don't linger on it enough. **Add sensory detail:** *"Imagine standing as a stone for centuries, watching your descendants walk past, unable to cry out."* Also, the "elderly people could tell the story...during the trip they could understand the areas" section is abstract—give a concrete example of a parent pointing to a mountain and saying, *"That's your great-grandmother."*

## 5. Filler Words & Hesitations

**High-frequency patterns:**
- **"uh" / "um"**: Extremely frequent (50+ instances)—used almost as punctuation.
- **"I mean"**: Overused as a filler (15+ times) when you're searching for words.
- **"so" / "and so"**: Repetitive sentence starters that signal uncertainty.
- **"yeah, yeah, yeah"**: Repetitive affirmation in the Q&A section (understandable in conversation but detracts in a presentation).
- **Word repetitions/false starts**: "They are, they are..." / "The story, the story..."

**Concrete strategies:**
1. **Pause instead of filling:** When you feel "uh" coming, close your mouth and pause silently for 1 second. Silence conveys confidence; "uh" signals hesitation.
2. **Rehearse transitions:** Your "I mean" often appears when pivoting ideas—script these transitions: *"Here's the strange part..."* / *"This raises a question..."*
3. **Slow down:** You rush through complex ideas, triggering fillers. Practice delivering one idea per breath.

## 7. Sentence Variety & Flow

**Issue: Choppy, fragmented sentences with excessive conjunctions.**

Example cluster:  
*"So their behaviors… Finally, makes them become the two mountains. It's really weird, right? Because, I mean, something in the local square mesh legend. They were called as this or the transfers."*

**Analysis:** These are fragments and run-ons strung together with "so," "because," "I mean." The rhythm is staccato and disjointed.

**Suggested revision:**  
*"Their actions ultimately transformed them into the two mountains—a truly strange concept. In Squamish legend, beings known as Transformers possessed this power."*  
(Two clear sentences: one compound, one simple. Removes filler, improves flow.)
